Some evidently drunk girl approached me and asked me what I’m all dressed up for (lilac shirt). I said I was at this recital a few hours ago. She said I’m sorry? I said there was this concert; it was not very bad at all; there is no need to be sorry. She said, oh, cool, that’s crazy. I said I wouldn’t use that epithet. She said something about another concert (a noisy thing that took place several days ago). I understood decidedly nothing she was saying, so I just cracked a few jokes to some of her words. Are those things expensive? Cufflinks, no. Then she started talking about some *Homo Americanus*, an entity (a Kevin) with whom she had what one can only assume to be sex (‘we explored a few positions’ was amongst her frightful phraseologies) in an automobile before the concert concluded. I said, much too kind of you to divulge this intelligence. She said sorry, the music, what? I said, no, nothing. Say, what do they do to turn chicken into turkeys? She chuckled, and said, oh, your accent. Wait, what is your name? Your accent is so cute. Is it British? Acute ear, I said. Thanks, she said, Kevin said the same thing. Are you British? No, not at all. I had a stroke when I was six—there was football, there was tea—and I woke up like this. She looked concerned for a bit, but quickly she dismissed it. Do you have a car? I refrained myself from acting according to my will (I did however concede to my lesser volition of finishing the hopeless liquid in my red plastic cup in a gulp), and, smiling, do excuse me, I must get something more to drink. I’m awfully sorry. I don’t have a car. I have however had a pleasant time talking to you. My name is Chris. What is your name? A most beautiful name—Chris is boring. No, I’m afraid I’m not in need of a new name; I don’t mind boring. My friend there, right there, might be able to offer you a ride; he’s right over there, the one with the moustache. A pair of western boots who was evidently her friend marched to us at this convinient juncture, and ready compliments started erupting into my unready ears. I took the opportunity and dashed for the exit. I left an open bar behind completely sober. Sobriety, one suspects, is just being fully conscious of one’s own escape velocity.

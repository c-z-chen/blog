吾忆昔得太行山，一场差样：天色漭漭荡荡，路遥跷跷峃峃；碎石里欲倒不倒，悬崖处踉踉跄跄；投至下得山来，直得魂飞胆丧。

I do recollect in days of yore, when I acquired you upon the Taihang Mountains, a spectacle of surpassing singularity: the firmament hung vast and vacillating in its turbid immensity; the path stretched prodigiously precipitous and protuberant with peril. Amidst the fragmented lapilli you were ever on the verge of collapse yet resolutely upright; upon the vertiginous precipices you did stagger and stumble in most pitiable ataxia. When at length we descended from that formidable eminence, my guts took flight and my galls were utterly annihilated.

又忆得向阳子江边，不肯上舩：千堆万托，向后向前；两耳卓朔，四蹄拳挛。教人随后行棓，吾乃向前自撁。烂缰绳一拽拽断，穷醋大一闪闪翻。踏碎艎板，筑损舩舷；蘸湿鞋底，砦破衫肩。更被傍人大笑，弄却多小酸寒。

I do recollect also your recalcitrance by the sunward banks of the Yangzi, where you did obstinately refuse to embark upon the vessel: a thousand importunities and ten thousand persuasions availed nought; you oscillated anteriorly and posteriorly, your two auricles pricked in rigid alarm, your four hooves contracted in convulsive refusal. When men were dispatched to drive you from behind with staves, I advanced to haul you forward by your own halter myself. Lo, the rotten cordage, wrenched with a single vehement traction, ruptured asunder; this penurious pedant I, in one precipitous flash, was toppled over. You pulverised the deck-planks beneath your hooves, fractured the gunwales of the bark; I saturated my soles and macerated the shoulders of my garment. Furthermore derided by the spectators, I was rendered an object of manifold frigid mockery and acidulous mortification.

吾乃私心有约，报汝勤恪：待吾立功立事，有官有爵，虽然好马到来，也不撁汝买却。遣汝向朱门里出入，瓦宅里跳跃。更拟别买猪皮，换却朽烂绳索，觅新鞍子以俻，求好龓头与着。准拟同受荣华，岂料中途疾作。

Yet in my private cogitation I formed a covenant, promising you due recompense for your assiduous fidelity: When I shall have established merit and accomplished great affairs, attaining office and dignity, though the finest steeds of the realm be proffered, I shall not suffer you to be bartered away. I shall dispatch you to ingress and egress the vermilion gates of noble houses, to gambol and caper within tile-roofed mansions. Moreover, I shall procure fresh porcine hide to supplant your decayed harness, seek out a new and commodious saddle for your preparation, and obtain an excellent headstall for your adornment. Together we were to partake of prosperity and prestige—yet who could have prognosticated that a mortal malady would interpose itself midway?

呜呼，道路茫茫，赖汝相将；疲羸若此，行李交妨。肋底气脋脋，眼中泪汪汪；草虽嫩而不食，豆虽多而不尝。

Alack! The highways of this sublunary realm are vast and labyrinthine; I relied upon you as my faithful companion and coadjutor. Now, enervated and emaciated to such a degree, you impede even the most exiguous baggage. Your ribs heave with laborious respiration; your eyes overflow with lachrymose inundation. Though the grass be tender, you consume it not; though the beans be abundant, you taste them not.

小童子凌晨报来，道汝昨夜身亡。汝虽殒薨，吾亦悲伤：数年南北，同受恓惶；觔疲力尽，冒雪冲霜。今则长辞木橙，永别麻缰。破龓头抛在墙边，任从风雨；鞔鞍子弃于槽下，更不形相。

The young page announced at the auroral hour that you had perished during the preceding night. Though you are departed and defunct, I too am oppressed by sorrow: for several years, traversing north and south, we endured vicissitudes and tribulations in common; you exhausted sinew and strength, braving snow and frost. Now you have forever relinquished the wooden manger and made eternal separation from the hempen halter. Your dilapidated headstall lies discarded by the wall, abandoned to the caprices of wind and rain; your worn saddle is cast beneath the feeding trough, nevermore to be beheld.

念汝畜类之中，实堪惊诃。生不逢时，来于我舍；在家时则小刨小刷，趂程时则连明至夜。

Considering you among the brute creation, verily you do merit astonishment and laudation. Born in an inopportune juncture, you came into my humble habitation. At home you submitted to negligible curryings and brushings; upon the road you laboured from dawn until the depths of night.

胡不生于王武子之时，必爱能鸣；胡不生方汉灵帝之时，定将充驾；胡不如卫懿公之鹤，猷得乘轩；胡不如曹不兴之蝇，尚蒙图写。

Why were you not foaled in the age of Wang Wuzi, when your neighing should be cherished? Why not in the era of Emperor Ling of Han, that you should have the honour to draw the imperial chariot? Why not as those cranes of Duke Yi of Wei, that you should have been seated in the carriage? Why not as that fly of Cao Buxing, that you should have been immortalised in painting?

若比彼为龙被酰，为龟被刳，为虵受戮，为马遭屠；尚得卒于槽下，念汝必保微躯。

If compared to the dragon mashed to mince meat, the tortoise eviscerated, the serpent slaughtered, or the horse butchered—at least you attained your end beneath the manger, and I take comfort that your modest frame shall remain intact.

《书》云：幤盖弗弃，为埋马也；幤帷弗弃，为埋狗也。《书》既不载埋驴途，乃付于屠者。

*The Book of Documents* declares: the chariot-covering shall not be discarded, for it is employed in the burial of horses; the chariot-curtain shall not be discarded, for it is employed in the burial of dogs. Since the *Documents* make no provision for the interment of asses, I consign you to the hands of the butcher.

汝若来生作人，还来近我。若更为驴，莫驮醋大。出门则路即千里万里，程粮贱无十个五个；向屋檐下奇寄宿，破箩里盛锉；猛雪里虽行，深埿里虽过；爱把借人，更将撁磨；只解向汝背上吟诗，都不管汝膓中饥饿。

If in your next incarnation you should be reborn as a human, return once more into my proximity. If you are again engendered as an ass, bear not another pedant. When setting forth upon the road, the journey shall extend a thousand or ten thousand miles; provisions shall be meagre, scarce five or ten measures. You should seek precarious lodging beneath the eaves of houses, your provender placed in broken baskets. Though you must press onward through driving snow and traverse abyssal mire; though men shall borrow you freely and harness you to the millstone; though they shall do nought but compose verses upon your back, paying no heed to the famine gnawing within thy entrails—

教汝托生之处，凡有数般：莫生官人家，轭驮入长安；莫生军将家，打球力虽摊；莫生陆脚家，终日受皮鞭；莫生和尚家，道汝罪弥天。愿汝生于田舍家，且得共男女一般看。

I counsel you concerning the places of your metempsychosis, and there are several varieties to be eschewed: Be not born into the houses of bureaucratic ranks, lest you have to bear the yoke into Chang’an. Be not born into the families of military ranks, lest you exhaust your strength in polo. Be not born into the houses of foot-travellers, lest you endure the whip from dawn to dusk. Be not born into the dwellings of monks, lest they declare your sins offends the heaven. Rather, may you be born into a rustic farmhouse, where you shall be regarded with the same affection as they do their own sons and daughters.

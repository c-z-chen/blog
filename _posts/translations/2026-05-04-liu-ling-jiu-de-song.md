**《酒德颂》刘伶** <br />
有大人先生，以天地为一朝，万期为须臾。日月为扃牖，八荒为庭衢。行无辙迹，居无室庐。幕天席地，纵意所如。止则操卮执觚，动则挈榼提壶。唯酒是务，焉知其馀。有贵介公子，搢绅处士。闻吾风声，议其所以。乃奋袂攘襟，怒目切齿。陈说礼法，是非锋起。先生于是方捧罂承槽，衔杯漱醪。奋髯踑踞，枕麹藉糟。无思无虑，其乐陶陶。兀然而醉，豁尔而醒。静听不闻雷霆之声，熟视不睹泰山之形。不觉寒暑之切肌，利欲之感情。俯观万物，扰扰焉如江汉之载浮萍。二豪侍侧，焉如蜾蠃之与螟蛉。

**Laude Virtutis Vini, by Liu Ling** <br />
There is a Great Man, a Gentleman, who holds the Heavens and Earth for one singular dawn, and the ten-thousand terms for a mere instant. The sun and moon are folding doors and lattice windows; the eight wastes are courts and thoroughfares. Going, he leaves no rut nor trace; dwelling, he has no chamber nor hut. The Heavens as curtain, the Earth as mat, his wills whithersoever it pleases he pursues. Resting, he grasps a cup and holds a goblet; stirring, he carries a flagon and lifts a tankard. Only unto wine is he devoted; aught of the rest, who knows? There are splendid scions, and grave girdled-gown gentlemen of retirement. Hearing the drift of my repute, they debate the wherefores of it. Thereupon they shake out sleeves, thrust up robes, glare with grim eyes, gnash with grinding teeth. They set forth and declare rites and statutes; rights and wrongs rise keen-edged. The Gentleman thereupon is just then clasping an earthen jar and receiving the flowing vat; with cup in mouth he rinses with the lees. Beard bristling, he squats astride; the yeast his pillow, he reclines upon the dregs. By no thought, by no care, his joy is deep and full. In a stupor, he is drunk; all at once, wide and clear he awakes. Stilly he listens, and hearkens not the sound of thunder; fixedly he looks, and sees not the form of Mount Tai. He feels not the keen bite of cold and heat upon his flesh, nor the stirring of profit and desire upon his affections. Bending, he beholds all mortal things, troubling and thronging in their restless turmoil, as if they were duckweed borne upon the Yangtze and the Han Rivers. The two grand heroes standing attendant at his side, are to him as the wasp and the caterpillar’s young.
